Forward Commitments for Builders: Cost, ROI and How to Compete With National Builders in 2026

EducationNovember 18, 2026By Brandon Crysler

National builders have quietly sold most of their homes with forward commitments for years. Here is what a forward commitment costs, how the math compares with a price cut, and how a local or regional builder can set one up in 2026.

Key takeaways

  • A forward commitment is a builder-paid agreement that reserves a pool of mortgage money at a fixed, below-market rate for any qualified buyer who closes within a set window.
  • It is the big builders' main sales tool. AEI research found about 64% of new homes sold by the largest builders used a permanent rate buydown, compared with about 13% for smaller builders.
  • It usually costs less than a price cut. Industry research estimates that lowering a buyer's rate by 1 percentage point costs a builder about 3.2% of the price, while a similar payment drop through a price cut takes about 10%.
  • It protects your comps because the list price, and the recorded sale price, stay the same.
  • Local builders can use it too. A bank lender can size a commitment to a realistic number of closings instead of requiring a national-builder volume.

What is a forward commitment? (Short answer)

A forward commitment is an agreement between a home builder and a mortgage lender in which the builder pays a fee up front to reserve a block of mortgage financing at a fixed interest rate below the current market. Every qualified buyer who finances through that lender and closes inside the commitment window gets the reserved rate for the full term of the loan, even if market rates rise in the meantime. Researchers sometimes call it a bulk forward commitment or a permanent buydown, because the lower rate lasts for the life of the loan rather than one or two years.

If you want the basics first, start with our earlier article, Forward Commitments: Why They Give Builders an Advantage. This guide goes deeper into the cost, the numbers and how to set one up.

The 2026 new-home market by the numbers

Builders are under more pressure to discount than at any point since the pandemic. According to the National Association of Home Builders/Wells Fargo Housing Market Index released November 17, 2026:

  • Builder confidence fell to 25, the lowest reading since April 2012.
  • 46% of builders cut prices in November 2026, the highest share in the post-Covid period, with an average cut of 6%.
  • 68% of builders used sales incentives, also a post-Covid high.

On a $350,000 home, a 6% price cut is $21,000 off the top, recorded publicly as a lower sale price. That is the cost a forward commitment is designed to beat.

Why national builders use forward commitments and most local builders don't

The largest builders own mortgage companies, close thousands of loans a year and can buy commitments in very large blocks. That scale is why the share of homes sold with a permanent buydown is so much higher for national builders than for smaller ones. Smaller builders have usually been left with two weaker tools: cutting the price or offering a temporary 2-1 buydown.

The gap is about access, not about whether the tool works. When a lender sizes a commitment to your realistic closings over the next build cycle, instead of a national-builder volume, a builder selling a handful of homes a quarter can offer buyers the same kind of fixed below-market rate the big names advertise.

What does a forward commitment cost?

Pricing depends on how far below market you want the rate, the loan types, the size of the pool and how long the window stays open. As a rule of thumb from published research:

  • Lowering a buyer's rate by 1 percentage point costs a builder roughly 3.2% of the sales price, while matching that payment with a price cut takes about 10% (AEI Housing Center research, reported by HousingWire).
  • Across builder buydowns, an average rate discount of about 1.3 percentage points has been estimated to cost around 5% of the mortgage amount.

Those are industry estimates, not a quote. Your actual cost is set when the commitment is priced, which is why the first step is running the numbers on your own price points.

Forward commitment vs. 2-1 buydown vs. price cut vs. closing-cost credit

Through our Builder Advantage Program, the forward commitment does not count toward FHA's 6% seller concession limit, so a builder can still contribute toward the buyer's closing costs and prepaids on top of the lower rate.

How to set up a forward commitment in five steps

  1. List your inventory and pipeline. Count the homes you expect to close in the next 6 to 12 months, by price point and likely loan type (FHA, VA, USDA or conventional).
  2. Pick a target rate. Decide what payment you want to advertise. The lower the rate, the higher the cost, so compare two or three rate options side by side.
  3. Size the pool to realistic closings. Reserve what you are likely to use, not a best-case number.
  4. Market the payment, not the price. Put the rate and an example payment, with the required disclosures, on your signs, listings, model-home materials and agent outreach.
  5. Route buyers to one lender. The committed rate is only available through the lender holding the commitment, so a single point of contact keeps closings on schedule.

Eight questions to ask a lender before you sign

  1. What is the total cost, and how is it calculated: per loan, per pool or as a percentage of the loan amount?
  2. How long is the commitment window, and what happens to buyers whose homes finish late?
  3. What happens to unused funds when the window closes?
  4. Which loan types are eligible: FHA, VA, USDA, conventional?
  5. Are there credit score, debt-to-income or property overlays on the committed rate?
  6. Does the commitment count toward seller concession limits?
  7. Who is my point of contact, and how quickly can my sales team get a buyer pre-approved?
  8. What co-branded marketing and payment comparisons will you provide?

Who a forward commitment fits, and who it doesn't

A good fit: production and semi-custom builders with several homes to sell in the next year, spec homes sitting finished or near finished, communities where buyers are mostly payment-sensitive first-time and move-up buyers, and builders who have already cut prices once and don't want to do it again.

Probably not a fit: a single custom home with a cash buyer, or a buyer who will finance with a different lender of their own choosing. In those cases a closing-cost credit or a one-off buydown may be simpler.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a forward commitment and a permanent buydown?

They describe the same result from two angles. A permanent buydown is a rate that stays lower for the life of the loan. A forward commitment is the way builders buy it: by reserving a pool of financing at that rate up front for many buyers at once.

Is a forward commitment cheaper than cutting the price?

Usually, yes. Published research estimates that lowering a rate by 1 percentage point costs about 3.2% of the price, while a price cut needs about 10% to give the buyer the same payment. A price cut also lowers your comps; a forward commitment does not.

Can small builders get a forward commitment?

Yes. Large builders dominate this tool because of scale, but a lender can size a commitment to a smaller builder's realistic closings. Southern Home Loans sets them up for local and regional builders.

Does the buyer have to use the builder's lender?

To get the committed rate, yes. The rate lives with the lender that holds the commitment. Buyers can still choose another lender, but they would get that lender's market rate instead.

What happens if mortgage rates drop below the committed rate?

The committed rate is designed to sit below market. If market rates fall far enough, the commitment's advantage shrinks, which is why window length and pool size matter when you set one up.

Does a forward commitment affect the appraisal?

The home is sold at its full list price, so the recorded sale price is not reduced the way a price cut would be. Builder-paid financing costs are disclosed in the transaction as required.
